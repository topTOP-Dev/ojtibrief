# Control of Departing Aircraft

## Information provided to arrival

1. Runway to be used
2. Surface wind direction and speed, including significant variations therefrom.
3. QNH
4. Air Temparature for the runway to be used
5. Visibility, if less than 10 km
6. The correct time.

## Standard Clearance Departure

### Example phraseologies:

> **Chiang Mai Ground:** Cleared to Suvarnabhumi Aerodrome via **PANTA2N** departure, Flight Plan Route, 11 000 FEET, Squawk 4-4-4-4, [CTOT or TSAT].
> **Pilot:** (Readback)
> **Chiang Mai Ground:** Correct, Departure Runway 36, QNH 1-0-0-7, Time (correct time), [CTOT or TSAT].

## VFR Traffic Information

### Prior to taxi, pilots are to call “Chiang Mai Ground” for providing the following departing VFR flight information:

1. Intended route - Outbound Radial
2. Cruising altitude
3. SSR Code, if any

> **RPT081:** 3-6-0 radial, 15 NM, 3 500 FT

> **TWR:** Outbound radial 3-6-0, ** maintain 1 600 FT initially, expect higher after cross 10 NM **, transponder 3-3-6-7 (Assigned from TopSky ATC)

## CTOT

[MATS II, 2.5.4, 5) - iii) and iv)]

### To facilitate the efficient flow of traffic, departures with CTOT may be re-sequence with the following conditions:

1. All concerned flights must be in issued CTOTs;
2. As long as concerned flights are in their own CTOT windows. **The ATCOs may re-sequence issuing pushback clearance** which is not along with the order of CTOT.

### ATFM measures are exempted from the following considerations:

1. Experiencing an emergency, including aircraft subjected to unlawful interference;
2. In search and rescue or firefighting missions
3. Urgent medical evacuation flights specifically declared by medical authorities where flight delays would put the life of patients at risk;
4. VIP flights;
5. Specifically identified by appropriate authorities;
6. State and Military aircraft operating time-sensitive and/or critical missions;

### When prevailing traffic conditions, either in the air or on ground conditions, cause the concerned flights are not able to comply with such measures. ATFMU shall be notified of the reasons.

## Departure Sequence

### The ATCOs shall clear departing aircraft in the order in which they are ready for take-off, except those deviations may be made from this order of priority to facilitate the maximum number of departures with the least average delay.

### Factors which should be considered in relation to the departure sequence include, inter alia:

1. types of aircraft and their relative performance;
2. routes to be followed after take-off;
3. any specified minimum departure interval between take-offs;
4. need to apply wake turbulence separation minima;
5. aircraft which should be afforded priority; and
6. aircraft subject to ATFM requirements.

#### Note

In case that the aircraft is not comply with their CTOT windows. That should allow the aircraft takes off to falicitate the flow of traffic.

### Based on local agreements

1. The aircraft should be released APP **before entering runway** but It could be allowed to line-up before released from APP as well.
2. TWR shall ensure that departures will commit the time interval:

   | Same Performance | 2 minutes | A320 -> A320, A320 -> B738 |
   | Slower follows Faster | 1 minute | AT76 -> A320, DC3T -> B738 |
   | Faster follows Slower| 4 minute | A320 -> AT76, TEX2 (AT6TH) -> DC3T |

Unless, coordination from APP has been made.

## Take Off Clearance

### Prior to issuing take-off clearance, the ATCOs shall scan, visually, the runway to be used in accordance with the Working Instruction of Scanning the Vicinity of Aerodrome and Manoeuvring Area.

## Conditional Clearance

### Conditional phrases, **behind landing aircraft or after departing aircraft**, shall not be used for movements affecting the active runway(s), **except when the aircraft and vehicles concenrned are seen by the appropriate controller and pilot**. The aircraft or vehicle causing the condition in the clearance issued shall be the first aircraft/vehicle to pass in front of the other aircraft concerned. In all cases a conditional clearance shall be given in the following order and consist of:

| Item                               | Example                         |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Identification                     | AIQ3444                         |
| The Condition                      | Behind Traffic [On Short Final] |
| The clearance                      | Liup up [and wait] Behind       |
| Brief reiteration of the condition | (optional)                      |

#### Example phraseology:

> **TWR:** AIQ3444, Traffic B738 on short final, **Report (when) traffic insight**.
> **AIQ3444:** Traffic insight.
> **TWR:** AIQ3444, Behind Landing traffic on short final, Line up and wait behind.

#### Note

This implies the need for the aircraft receiving the conditional clearance to identify the aircraft or vehicle causing the conditional clearance.

### When an ATC clearance is required prior to takeoff, **the take-off clearance shall not be issued until the ATC clearance has been transmitted to and acknowledged by the aircraft concerned.** The ATC clearance shall be forwarded to the aerodrome control tower with the least possible delay after receipt of a request made by the tower or prior to such request if practicable.

### **The take-off clearance shall be issued when the aircraft**:

- **is ready for take-off**,
- **at or approaching the departure runway, and**
- **the traffic situation permits (See next section).**

To reduce the potential for misunderstanding, the take-off clearance shall include the designator of the departure runway.

#### Example phraseology:

> EVA258, Chiang Mai Tower, **Hold short of Runway 36, Report Ready for departure.**

> THA111, Chiang Mai Tower, **Hold short of Runway 36, Report when ready (for departure), Expect No.1, Traffic A320 20 NM**

### When there is ongoing local traffic (helicopter), The traffic information shall be included to take-off clearance. The traffic information should contain:

1. Description of aircraft / Nature of traffic
2. Current position
3. Direction of flight
4. Proposed altitude

#### Example phraseology:

> BKP216, **Traffic Helicopter 8 NM North of aerodrome, Northbound, Altitude not above 1 600 FT**, After departure, Climb via SID, remain TWR frequency, (wind), RWY36, Cleared for take-off.

> CAL852, **Traffic AT6 holding on Right Outside Downwind, Altitude 2 100 FT**, After departure, Climb via SID, remain TWR frequency, (wind), RWY36, Cleared for take-off.

## Separation of departing aircraft

### A departing aircraft will not normally be permitted to commence take-off **until:**

1. The preceding departing aircraft **has crossed the end** of the runway-in-use, or
2. **has started a turn**, or
3. until all preceding landing aircraft are **clear of the runway-in-use**.

## Lateral Separation

### [MATS I, 5.4.1.2.6] Separation methods: Separate aircraft by one of the following methods:

1. Clear aircraft on different airways or routes whose widths or protected airspace do not overlap.
2. Clear aircraft below Flight level 180 to proceed to and report over or hold at different geographical locations determined visually or by reference to NAVAIDs.
3. Clear aircraft to hold over different fixes whose holding pattern airspace areas do not overlap each other or other airspace to be protected.
4. **Clear departing aircraft to fly specified headings which diverge by at least 45 degrees.**

### Geographical Separation

1. indicated by position reports over different geographical locations that have been specified in MATS Part 2 as being separated; and
2. constant or increasing.
   ![Separation Rules Illustration](images/lateralsep1.png)

### Separation between Arriving aircraft and an aircraft taking off

1. Lateral separation is considered to exist between an arriving aircraft that subsequently commenced final approach, and a departing aircraft that has been cleared on **a segregated flight path**.
2. For this purpose, a segregated flight path is considered to exist when the departing aircraft **will not be manoeuvring within 45 degrees either side of the reciprocal of the final approach path** while the arriving aircraft is on the final approach track.
3. Except as specified in 2. above, take-offs shall not be permitted after an arriving aircraft has commenced final approach until:

- it is sighted by the tower controller and reasonable assurance exists that a landing can be accomplished; or
- separation standards can be applied between an arriving aircraft which misses its approach and an aircraft desiring take-off clearance.

4. In the application of this standard, the controller must estimate that the required separation will exist at the time the take-off is commenced, and ATS surveillance system observation or DME report must be used to confirm that separation is not infringed.

## Transfer of Control

1. Immediatly after airborne.

   > After Departure Contact Approach 129.6, [Wind Information], RWY36, Cleared for Take-Off.

2. Clear of local traffic.

   > **TWR :** Traffic Light aircraft 5 miles east joining right downwind RWY36 2,100 feet, After departure Climb via SID to 11,000 feet, **Report Passing 3,100 feet**
   > **Pilot :** (Readback)
   > **TWR :** (When Airborne or After Departure) Remain Tower Frequency, [Wind Information], RWY36, Cleared for Take-Off.

   > **TWR :** Traffic Helicopter 5 miles east, **fly Northbound on Helicopter Route**, After departure Climb via SID to 11,000 feet, Remain Tower Frequency, [Wind Information], RWY36, Cleared for Take-Off.

3. In the event of Hi-Key
   > Traffic **Aircraft Holding over aerodrome 5,500 feet, After departure Climb via SID to 4,500 feet, Report over CC201**, Remain Tower Frequency, [Wind Information], RWY36, Cleared for Take-Off.
