# Control of Arriving Aircraft

## Transfer of communication

### APP shall transfer communication to TWR:

1. **IFR** at LASOX.
2. **VFR** at 10 NM, or the point that the pilot can visual the aerodrome (Aerodrome insight) which is not further than 10 NM.

## Information provided to arrival

1. (Instructions to circuit), Runway to be used
2. Surface wind direction and speed, including significant variations therefrom.
3. QNH

## Example phraseologies:

> ** Join Right Downwind Runway 36 ** , Wind 2-8-0 degrees 5 khots, QNH 1-0-0-5. Expect Number 2 after traffic A320 20 NM.

> ** Join 5 NM Initial ** , Wind 2-8-0 degrees 5 khots, QNH 1-0-0-5. Report 5 NM Initial.

> ** Join Helicopter Route at San Klang** , 1 600 feet, Report over San Klang.

> ** Join Right Downwind Runway 36 and Hold **, Wind, QNH.

> ** Orbit Left on Right Downwind** , expect Number 3 after traffic A333 30 NM.

> After San Klang, **Join Right Downwind Runway 36 (or Join 1 mile east of aerodrome)**, Standby for crossing runway.

# Separation

## Separation of landing aircraft and preceding landing aircraft and departing aircraft using the same runway.

### a landing aircraft will not normally be permitted to cross the runway threshold on its final approach **until:**

1. The preceding departing aircraft **has crossed the end** of the runway-in-use, or
2. **has started a turn**, or
3. until all preceding landing aircraft are **clear of the runway-in-use**.

![Separation Rules Illustration](images/Separation1.png)

## Reduction in separation minima in the vicinity of aerodromes

### [MATS I, 6.1] The separation minima detailed in Chapter 5, 5.4.1 (Lateral) and 5.4.2 (Longitudinal), may be reduced in the vicinity of aerodromes if:

1. adequate separation can be provided by the aerodrome controller when each aircraft is continuously visible to this controller; or
2. each aircraft is continuously visible to flight crews of the other aircraft concernedand the pilots thereof report that they can maintain their own separation; or
3. in the case of one aircraft following another, the flight crew of the succeeding aircraft reports that the other aircraft is in sight and separation can be maintained.

## Visual Separation

### [MATS I, 5.9.1.1] Visual separation may be achieved:

1. by the use of visual procedures; or
2. by assigning visual separation responsibility to a pilot.
   [MATS I, 5.9.1.3] ATC may assign to the pilot of one aircraft responsibility to maintain separation with another aircraft only if:
3. The aircraft to be separated are operating at or below 10,000 ft; and
4. The pilot has:

- reported the other aircraft in sight; and
- accepted responsibility to follow, or maintain his or her own separation with, that aircraft.

## Separation using visual observation

### When weather conditions permit, the aerodrome controller may provide separation **based on visual observations as coordinated with Approach Control** provided that:

1. the aerodrome controller is in agreement and accepts responsibility for the provision of such visual control;
2. where required, the aircraft concerned are on the aerodrome control frequency;
3. where required, specific airspace is released to the aerodrome controller for the purpose of providing such control.

### Aerodrome controllers may also separate by the use of visual observation of aircraft position and projected flight paths.

### When aircraft are operating visually as aerodrome traffic, ATC shall issue one or more of the following:

1. clearances designed to maintain separation;
2. sequencing instructions;
3. relevant traffic information.

### Pilots shall be advised of their number in the landing sequence to assist in identification of traffic.

### ATC shall maintain, as far as possible, a continuous visual watch to detect and determine the position, and ensure the safety of, aircraft.

# Clearance to land

## Prior to issuing landing clearance the ATCOs shall scan, visually, the runway to be used in accordance with the Working Instruction of Scanning the Vicinity of Aerodrome and Manoeuvring Area

## An aircraft may be cleared to land when there is reasonable assurance that the separation will exist when the aircraft crosses ** the runway threshold **

## Clearance to land shall not be issued before:

1. the aircraft has established final approach course of an in instrument approach; or
2. the aircraft has been sighted by the tower controller:

- on downwind leg of the circuit pattern; or
- on base leg; or
- on final

## Observation by radar satisfies th sighting requirement.

## When the landing area is occupied by another aircraft or is obstructed, arriving aircraft may be issued with a clearance to:

1. **continue approach** if there is no immediate assurance that the landing areas will become available. This shall be followed by the appropriate clearance; or
2. **go around, or orbit** if in a position to do so, should the landing area not be available. When required, a clearance to commence a second approach or hold shall follow these instructions. The nature of the obstruction shall be advised if not apparent to the approaching aircraft.

## Missed Approach Procedures

- [MATS II, 6.2] TWR shall ensure that the missed approach aircraft is separated from all flights operating in aerodrome traffic circuit. Once upon local flights are being carried out, the phraseology described below should be given to the aircraft which suspects to make or make missed approach: ** CONTINUE RUNWAY HEADING TO 4 000 FEET **

### Note:

Unless, otherwise coordination with APP. APP will provide the instruction to missed approach aircraft other than the procedure mentioned above.

### Example Phraseologies:

> Traffic on right downwind RWY36 2,500 feet, **In case of Missed Approach, Continue Runway Heading to 4,000 feet**.

After coordination:

> **In case of Missed Approach, Turn right heading 060 climb 7,000 feet**
